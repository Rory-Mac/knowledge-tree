Agency

The universe, i.e. all that exists and is perceived to exist, can be conceived of as having a state, a clock, a rule-set dictating how the state of the universe changes from time-step to time-step. Determinism is the view that the universe is deterministic, i.e. that the state of the universe at a given time-step is dictated entirely by its state in its previous time-step. This is equivalent to the view that the rule-set of the universe is determinisitc. This is contrasted to non-determinism, or indeterminism, or stochasticism: the view that the rule-set of the universe is non-deterministic, i.e. that the evolution of state form time-step to time-step involves a degree of randomness.

Observers observe alternate resolutions of the universe. An experiment is a narrowing of the universe to some observable resolution, that is, isolation of a subset of its state across a subset of time such that the partial rule-set by which that subset of state is governed can be observed. The experiment produces an observed outcome. The partial rule-set is inferred from state and outcome. The partial rule-set is stable until a repeat of the same experiment yields a distinct partial rule-set, which can then be merged with the pre-existing set.

In a deterministic universe, an experiment at any resolution yields a singular outcome, which is the same outcome no matter how many times the experiment is repeated. In a non-deterministic universe, an experiment may yield different outcomes when repeated, deriving a non-deterministic rule-set. Contradiction across experimental outcomes require additional experiments. Across this experimental process, the observer's derived rule-set may oscillate between determinism and non-determinism.

If the universe is non-deterministic, it can be interpreted that the branches along which the universe can evolve that are causally independent from one another, exist simultaneously, or it can be interpreted that this set of branches collapse into a singular branch that exists and is determined by chance. The empirical belief views the observer as being without agency in all above scenarios, given the observer exists within the observed. Whether the universe is deterministic or non-deterministic, it is governed by a rule-set that predetermines the observer's existence and observation. This holds true whether the rule-set is deterministic, non-deterministic, or truly random.

The idealist perspective instead asserts that a causal relationship exists between the observer and the observed. Within this perspective, the observed partial rule-set is in some way a product of the belief by which it is observed. Thus we can imagine an observer transform deterministic structures into non-deterministic structures and vice versa, as derived from belief. This does not resolve the question of agency however, which requires us to answer the question of whether the belief is generated in a deterministic or non-deterministic fashion.

If the observer holds the belief that the rule-set is deterministic, than it is, or if non-deterministic, than it isn't. If the observer were to observe all rule-sets and all beliefs, the observer would observe the deterministic path that involves the observer's adoption and discard of beliefs that generate deterministic and non-deterministic systems, towards the desire that such omniscience produces.
