Fluid Mechanics

Fluid mechanics studies how the properties of fluid (density, viscosity, pressure and temperature) affect the mechanical behavior of fluid. Dynamic viscosity is given as the ratio of the fluid's shear stress (\( \tau \)) to its shear rate (\(\dot \gamma\)), where shear stress is the ratio of force to distance and shear rate the ratio of area to velocity. Kinematic viscosity relates viscosity to fluid density. \[ \eta = \frac{\tau}{\dot \gamma} = \frac{F/A}{v/d} = \frac{F \cdot d}{A \cdot v}\] \[ v = \frac{\eta}{p}\] Pascal's Law formulates that a pressure change in one area of a contained incompressible fluid is transmitted to every boundary point of that fluid's surface. \[ P = \frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{F_2}{A_2} \] Reynolds number predicts a fluid's characteristic flow as either laminar, turbulent, or transitional. It is given as the ratio of the product of the fluid's density, velocity and characteristic, to that fluid's viscosity. \[ Re = \frac{pvD}{\mu} \] Dominant inertial forces give a high Reynold number and predict turbulent flow. Dominant viscous forces give a low Reynold number and predict laminar flow.
